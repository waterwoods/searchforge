# Unified Intake Focus Mode + Customer-First Layout Sprint — Blueprint

**Sprint**: Unified Intake Focus Mode + Customer-First Layout  
**Date**: 2026-03-15  
**Budget**: 20–40 minutes  
**Scope**: Layout focus, product presentation, role clarity — no new features

---

## 1. Why This Sprint Matters Now

- Product logic is strong; page still feels like a lab/workbench surface
- Chen Kui trial needs a page that feels sellable and focused
- Current layout: dense nav, competing elements, unclear customer-vs-broker roles
- Commercial risk: prospect sees "one tool among many" instead of "this is the product"

---

## 2. Why Layout Focus Matters Commercially

- First impression in 10 seconds: does this look like a product or a lab?
- Customer Entry must feel like the obvious starting point for Chen Kui's customers
- Broker Workbench must feel like the office-side review surface
- Distraction = lost trust; focus = "this is built for me"

---

## 3. Why Customer-vs-Broker Role Clarity Matters

| Role | Who | Feel |
|------|-----|------|
| **Customer Entry** | Chen Kui's customer | Place to paste/enter message; start of intake; simple, obvious, prominent |
| **Broker Workbench** | Chen Kui's assistant/office | Place to review structured case; next move, collected, still needed, human confirmation |

Layout and UX must reinforce this distinction.

---

## 4. What "Good Enough" Layout Polish Looks Like

- Left nav reduced or hidden on Unified Intake so the page feels focused
- Customer Entry area is the visual hero: prominent, above the fold, obvious
- Tab labels and copy clarify: Customer Entry = customer-facing; Broker Workbench = office-facing
- Page feels like a product surface, not a lab tool
- No new features; no full redesign; high-ROI, low-risk changes only

---

*End of blueprint*
