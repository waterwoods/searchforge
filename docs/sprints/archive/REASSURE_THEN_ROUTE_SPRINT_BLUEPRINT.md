# Reassure-Then-Route Intake Sprint — Blueprint

**Sprint**: Reassure-Then-Route Intake  
**Date**: 2026-03-15  
**Budget**: 30–60 minutes  
**Scope**: Conversation pattern and customer feeling at intake — not full conversational AI redesign

---

## 1. Why Reassurance-First Intake Matters Now

- Current system feels mechanical: classify → ask follow-up → hand off
- Founder insight: customers should feel **understood, reassured, guided** — not like they're talking to a bot that only classifies
- Commercial risk: mechanical tone damages trust; reassurance-first builds it

---

## 2. Why This Is Commercially Important

- Chen Kui trial: first impression = trust
- Payment/claim/cancellation: high-emotion moments — answer first, then collect
- "Already sent" / "what do I do now": acknowledge before asking more
- Trust = retention; mechanical = churn

---

## 3. What "Good Enough" Looks Like

| Before | After |
|--------|-------|
| Ask for notice before saying what matters | Say what matters first, then ask for notice |
| Generic handoff | Explicit "office will confirm" when uncertain |
| Silent case creation | Clear handoff moment; broker sees when case is ready |
| Robotic phrasing | Human, office-natural tone |
| Over-collect early | Collect only next most useful thing |

---

## 4. In Scope

- Reply strategy: answer first, then collect
- Safer uncertainty language: "office will confirm" instead of vague
- High-emotion first-turn phrasing (payment, claim, cancellation)
- "Already sent" / "上周发过了" warmer acknowledgment
- Case-creation timing signal for UI (when to suggest creating case)
- Human wording in reply templates

---

## 5. Out of Scope

- Full conversational AI redesign
- New flows or categories
- LLM prompt overhaul
- Multi-turn memory / context engine
- Auth, billing, multi-tenant

---

*End of blueprint*
